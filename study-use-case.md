- I pray and meditate days in advance, asking for guidance and searching for mini-sermons to preach to myself or others. This allows me to wrestle with the concepts personally and experiment with communicating them in ways that are clear and impactful.
- Once I sit down to study, I brainstorm first. I might put all my ideas in a file or start a conversation with AI. Here is one such prompt:


- The next step is to just read through the suggestions or my notes until I see a wedge appear, one piercing and prevailing thought that is the most impactful. 
- I usually keep that brainstorm file as is and copy-paste it into a new file because I am about to rip out everything that isn't absolutely essential to that wedge. I might do this twice if I see two wedges I haven't decided on yet.
- Next, I just make deletes and move things around in an order that flows for me.
- Next, I spend some time refining my opening in order to answer the "Why should I listen to you?" question in the first 60 seconds.
- Now I reread the entire message with my opening to see if the points and order still make sense. I ask myself, "Is this all driving towards a sharp and inevitable point (wedge)?"
- At this point, I might add back in some of the content from the initial brainstorm to fill in if I feel some of that content strongly helps reinforce the main wedge without side-tracking. The goal is to paint the picture as perfectly as you can, then put the brush down. Don't allow pet points to distract from the beautiful picture you just put together. Simple and clear wins over eloquence or an abundance of clever one-liners.
- The discussion questions come next to last. I imagine asking the question; I ask myself, and if an answer does not readily come to mind, the question is not good for discussion. It might be good for reflection, but that's not the point. If you really can't think of a good discussion question, don't force it; just omit it and move on to the next point.
- While I am editing, I make things more spelled out than necessary. I write the stories in full like I'm teaching them. I remove any AI speak or grammar that might have snuck in from the brainstorm file. I make things sound natural, like I am saying them to a friend at a coffee shop.
- Every time I make major edits, I reread the whole message from the beginning to where I left off.
