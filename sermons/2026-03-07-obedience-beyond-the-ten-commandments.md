# Following the Way

---

## Intro

We tend to think of obedience as keeping the Ten Commandments — the dos and don'ts of the faith. But God has asked for something else—to walk with Him, because He is the Way. We walk with Him through obeying both His written Word and His personal will.

> *"I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me." (*John 14:6)

God asks Israel to return to Him, "The Ancient Way":

> "Return now every one from his evil way, and make your ways and your deeds good.” (Jeremiah 18:11, TS2009)

The response of the people is stubborn self-interest:

> "But they will say, 'It is useless! We will follow our own plans, and each of us will act according to the stubbornness of our own evil heart.'" (Jeremiah 18:12 paraphrase)

How did they get here?

> "Because My people have forgotten Me...the ancient paths..." (Jeremiah 18:15)

"The ancient paths" is a phrase used several times in Jeremiah, and it is a parallel of Jesus' claim that He is "The Way." He is the *Ancient Path*.

But because they despised the "Word of Yahweh," they also rejected the Ancient Path:

> "Behold, the **word of the LORD** is a reproach to them;  
> They have no delight in it." (Jeremiah 6:10)

God's invitation:

> "Stand in the **ways** and see,  
> And ask for the **old paths**, where the **good way** is,  
> And walk in it;  
> Then you will find **rest for your souls**.  
> But they said, 'We will not walk in it.'" (Jeremiah 6:16)

Notice the parallel in Matthew 11:

> "Come to Me, all *you* who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find **rest for your souls**." (Matthew 11:28–29)

The ancient paths. The good way. Rest for our souls. God offers all of it — and the people answered: *We will not.*

Israel forgot Yahweh:

> "Because My people have forgotten Me..." (Jeremiah 18:15)

Have we forgotten Him, too?

## Main Points

### 1. We've Redefined Obedience Too Narrowly

Most of us grade ourselves by what we *didn't* do wrong. But Scripture names a second category of sin that gets almost no attention — the sin of neglect or willful ignorance.

> "Therefore, to him who knows to do good and does not do it, to him it is sin." (James 4:17)

Obedience isn't the absence of wrongdoing — it's the active pursuit of what God has asked.

What commands in Scripture have we quietly reclassified as suggestions?

---

### 2. Obeying the Word of God

> "But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts." (Romans 13:14)

> "Flee sexual immorality. Every sin that a man does is outside the body, but he who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body. Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own? For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's." (1 Corinthians 6:18-20)

> "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God." (Philippians 4:6-7)

> "And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men." (Colossians 3:23)

Do we flee sin, or do we leave the door cracked for sin to come in? A cracked door isn't a boundary. It's an invitation.

---

### 3. Obeying the Will of God

> "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father in heaven." (Matthew 7:21)

This is one of the most sobering verses in Scripture. Jesus isn't describing pagans — He's describing people who called Him Lord, who prophesied, who cast out demons. The verdict against them wasn't that they worshipped Baal. It was that they never actually did the will of the Father.



---

### 4. Living by the Word of God

> "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God." (Matthew 4:4)
>
> "Every commandment which I command you today you must be careful to observe, that you may live and multiply, and go in and possess the land of which the Lord swore to your fathers. 
>
> And you shall remember that the Lord your God led you all the way these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you *and* test you, to know what *was* in your heart, whether you would keep His commandments or not. 
>
> So He humbled you, allowed you to hunger, and fed you with manna which you did not know nor did your fathers know, that He might make you know that man shall not live by bread alone; but man lives by every *word* that proceeds from the mouth of the Lord. 
>
> You should know in your heart that as a man chastens his son, *so* the Lord your God chastens you.
>
> Therefore you shall keep the commandments of the Lord your God, to walk in His ways and to fear Him." (Dt 8:1–6)

## Illustrations

### King Saul — The Danger of Partial Obedience (1 Samuel 15)

God gave Saul a clear and specific command: completely destroy the Amalekites. Saul destroyed most of them — but kept the king alive and spared the best livestock. When Samuel arrived and confronted him, Saul said: *"I have obeyed the voice of the Lord."*

He genuinely believed it. He'd done most of what God said. He even had a religious reason: "We kept the sheep to sacrifice them."

Samuel's response cuts:

> *"Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams."* (1 Samuel 15:22)

God called it rebellion. Not idolatry, not blasphemy — *rebellion*. Because Saul treated God's clear command as negotiable.

The modern version of Saul keeps the Ten Commandments but quietly renegotiates everything else.

---

### The Cracked Door

"Flee sexual immorality" doesn't say manage it, limit it, or stay nearby. *Flee* means run. Close the door and run.

But do we? Or do we crack the door — *just to see what's in there?* We rationalize: "I'll just watch." "I'll just scroll a little further." "It's not technically sin."

A cracked door isn't a boundary. It's an invitation. And the enemy will take it every time.

Romans 13:14 says to make *no* provision for the flesh. Not a little. None. God isn't asking us to manage temptation. He's asking us to starve it.

---

### The Sunday Report Card

What if God handed you a report card today — not just for what you didn't do wrong, but for every command He's actually given you?

- Did you make no provision for the flesh?
- Did you flee immorality?
- Were you anxious for nothing?
- Did you do your work heartily as unto the Lord?
- Are you walking in His will for your life?

How does the grade look when the full list is on the table?

## Other Scriptures

**Matthew 7:21** — "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father in heaven." *(Eternal life is tied to doing the Father's will — not confession alone)*

**Matthew 4:4** — "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God." *(We need the whole Word — not selective obedience)*

**Luke 6:46** — "But why do you call Me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do the things which I say?" *(Jesus asks this directly — the gap between title and obedience)*

**James 1:22** — "But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves." *(The self-deception of hearing without doing)*

**James 4:17** — "Therefore, to him who knows to do good and does not do it, to him it is sin." *(The sin of omission — what we fail to do is also sin)*

**Romans 12:2** — "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God." *(The renewed mind is the path to knowing His will)*

**Proverbs 3:5-6** — "Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths." *(God has a specific will — and it's found by seeking Him in every area)*

**1 Samuel 15:22** — "Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams." *(Partial obedience is not obedience — it's rebellion dressed as worship)*

**John 14:15** — "If you love Me, keep My commandments." *(Obedience is the language of love)*

## Possible Closing Questions (Reflection)

- Is there a door in your life that's cracked open — something you've been managing instead of fleeing?
- Are you doing your work, your relationships, your everyday life *heartily as unto the Lord* — or are you still performing for people?
- What is one specific thing God has been asking of you that you've been treating as optional?
- When did you last actively seek God's specific will for your life — not just what the Bible says in general, but what He's saying to *you*?
- If you stood before God today, could you say you've been a *doer* of His word — or only a hearer?
