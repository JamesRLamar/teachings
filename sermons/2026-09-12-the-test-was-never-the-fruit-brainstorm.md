# The Test Was Never the Fruit - Brainstorm

Follow-up to [Which Mark Do You Bear?](2026-09-05-which-mark-do-you-bear.md). Last week: everyone is marked on the hand (what we do) and the forehead (what we love), and the Sabbath is God's weekly rehearsal for the final test. This week: walk back through every test God has ever given His people and show that it was always the same test, and it was never a test of what they knew.

## Main Idea

**Golden Apple:** Every test God has ever given His people is the same test: not what we know in our minds, but whom we love in our hearts, and love is proven by whether His law is kept when it costs something.

Anchor text (the verse that says it outright):

> "And you shall remember that the LORD your God led you all the way these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you and test you, to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep His commandments or not." (Deuteronomy 8:2)

> "...you shall not listen to the words of that prophet or that dreamer of dreams, for the LORD your God is testing you to know whether you love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul." (Deuteronomy 13:3)

God defines His own test in two verses: it is a test of the **heart**, it is measured by the **commandments**, and what it is looking for is **love**. Heart, law, love: one thing, not three.

**Layers the message can move through:**

1. **The Mind vs. The Heart** - Eve knew the command word-for-word and recited it to the serpent. Knowing it did not pass the test. A church that can decode every trumpet and symbol in Revelation can still fail the only test that was ever given.
2. **The Fruit vs. The Love** - The forbidden thing (fruit, a brother's favor, manna on the seventh day, a golden image, a closed window) is never the point. It is the visible place where an invisible love is exposed. Whatever we reach for under pressure is what we love.
3. **Then vs. Now** - The test has never changed, only the costume. Eden, the field, the wilderness, Babylon, Rome, Nicaea, and Revelation 13 are one exam re-administered to every generation. We are taking it right now.

## Main Points

### 1. Eve knew the command and still failed the test, because the test was love

> "So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate." (Genesis 3:6)

> "Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world—the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life—is not of the Father but is of the world." (1 John 2:15-16)

Genesis 3:6 and 1 John 2:16 line up word for word: good for food (lust of the flesh), pleasant to the eyes (lust of the eyes), desirable to make one wise (pride of life). John's diagnosis is not "she was ignorant." It is "the love of the Father is not in him." Eve had a perfect theology exam score (Genesis 3:2-3) and a divided heart. Love of the world does not argue with love of God; it simply crowds it out.

**The first test in history was failed by someone who could quote the command perfectly, because God was never testing her memory; He was testing her love.**

**If you can quote the command and still reach for the fruit, what does that reveal about what you actually love?**

### 2. Cain, Abraham, and the manna prove the law was in the heart before it was on stone

> "So the LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry? And why has your countenance fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin lies at the door...'" (Genesis 4:6-7)

> "...not as Cain who was of the wicked one and murdered his brother. And why did he murder him? Because his works were evil and his brother's righteous." (1 John 3:12)

> "...because Abraham obeyed My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes, and My laws." (Genesis 26:5)

> "Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you... that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not.'" (Exodus 16:4)

No tablet said "do not murder" to Cain; the law he broke was written on his heart (Romans 2:15). John puts Cain in the chapter about love because hatred is the failed test of love. Abraham kept "My commandments" 430 years before Moses. And at the manna, before Sinai, before Moses wrote a word, God says "My law" and administers it through the seventh day. The test at the manna was not "do you know which day it is?" It was "do you trust Me enough to stop?"

**God tested Cain, Abraham, and Israel by a law that was already in the heart, which means the test has always been about what governs the heart, not what is stored in the head.**

**Where in your week does God hand you a "seventh-day" test: a place where stopping, trusting, or yielding costs you something you can see?**

### 3. Babylon tested worship and prayer, not doctrine

> "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-Nego answered and said to the king, 'O Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. If that is the case, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace... But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, nor will we worship the gold image which you have set up.'" (Daniel 3:16-18)

> "Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went home. And in his upper room, with his windows open toward Jerusalem, he knelt down on his knees three times that day, and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as was his custom since early days." (Daniel 6:10)

Nobody in Babylon asked the three what they believed about the image; they asked whether they would bow to it. Nobody asked Daniel what he thought about prayer; the decree tested who he would pray to. Every test in Daniel is a test of worship and devotion, which is the first commandment, which Jesus says is love (Matthew 22:37-38). And notice "as was his custom since early days": Daniel passed the test at the window because he had been at the window for decades when there was no test. Loving God in secret is how you pass the exam in public.

**The world's tests always land on worship and devotion, because the enemy knows what God is testing: not the contents of our minds but the direction of our hearts.**

**If a decree were signed tomorrow, what "custom since early days" would carry you through it, and what customs are missing?**

### 4. A church can know every symbol and lose the only thing being tested

> "I know your works, your labor, your patience, and that you cannot bear those who are evil. And you have tested those who say they are apostles and are not, and have found them liars... Nevertheless I have this against you, that you have left your first love." (Revelation 2:2-4)

> "And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge... but have not love, I am nothing." (1 Corinthians 13:2)

Ephesus was the doctrinally sharpest church in Revelation. They tested teachers. They knew their symbols. They could have taught the trumpets. And Jesus says: I have this against you. The final test in Revelation is worship (13:15), provision (13:17), and keeping the commandments of God (14:12). Not one of those is a knowledge test. The marked in Revelation 14:12 are described by what they keep and whom they trust, not by what they can explain.

**The greatest danger to a church that knows the prophecies is passing every exam except the one God is giving.**

**What would Jesus write to this church: "you have tested the teachers," or "you have kept your first love"? Can both be true of us at once?**

## Illustrations

- **The Open-Book Exam** (opening): Every student wants the open-book test. Eve had one. The command was one sentence long, she had memorized it, and she still failed, because God's exams are not open-book. They are open-heart. You can bring every note you have into the room, and it will not help you, because the question is not "what does it say?" but "whom do you love when it costs you?"
- **The Man with the Inkhorn, revisited** (bridge from last week): He did not mark the people who knew the abominations were wrong; he marked the ones who sighed and cried over them. Knowledge and grief are not the same thing. The whole city knew. Only a few loved.
- **Job's Fence** (maps to points 2 and 4; the strongest tie to last week's provision test): Satan's accusation in Job 1:9-11 is that Job only loves God because God has "made a hedge around him" and blessed his hands. Take the provision away and he will curse You to Your face. That is the beast's exact thesis in Revelation 13:17: cut off buying and selling and see who still loves God. Job's test was not theological; it was whether love survives when the fence comes down.
- **Daniel's Window** (point 3): A window opened every day for sixty years is not a protest; it is a habit of love. When the decree came, the window was already open. Persecution does not create faithfulness; it reveals it.
- **The Roman Test** (point 3, world history): Around AD 112 the governor Pliny wrote to Emperor Trajan explaining his method for identifying Christians: he had them invoke the gods, offer wine and incense before the emperor's image, and curse Christ, "none of which those who are really Christians can be forced to do." Rome did not test Christians on doctrine. It tested worship. It was Daniel 3 with a Latin accent. Polycarp (c. AD 155) was offered his life if he would only say "Caesar is Lord" and burn a pinch of incense. Eighty-six years of serving Christ answered for him.
- **Anathema, then and now** (point 4; the ironic reversal): In Daniel 3 a world ruler mandated veneration of a golden image under threat of death. In AD 787 the Second Council of Nicaea, under the authority of the empire, mandated veneration of images and pronounced anathema on those who refused. Six centuries earlier, Bishop Victor of Rome (c. AD 190) tried to cut off the churches of Asia for keeping Passover on the fourteenth of Nisan rather than the Roman Sunday; Irenaeus and others rebuked him for it. The Council of Laodicea (c. AD 364) declared anathema on Christians who "rest on the Sabbath." The costume changed from a king's furnace to a bishop's decree, but it is the same test: worship and the commandments of God, under threat. The test did not end when the church grew; it moved inside the church.
- **The Maccabean Mother** (point 3; extra-biblical Jewish history): Under Antiochus IV (167 BC), Sabbath and circumcision were outlawed and Jews were commanded to eat swine's flesh. Old Eleazar and a mother with seven sons chose torture over one bite (2 Maccabees 6-7). Antiochus did not care what they believed about pork. He was testing whether God's law was in their hearts. It was.
- **Jesus in the Wilderness, the Test Eve Failed** (closing turn): Matthew 4 is Genesis 3 re-taken. Bread (flesh), kingdoms of the world (eyes), the pinnacle of the temple (pride). Jesus answers all three by quoting Deuteronomy 6 and 8, the very passages that define God's test as a test of the heart. He passed Eve's test in Eve's three categories, and He passed it as the loving Son, not the informed scholar. The second Adam did not out-know the serpent; He out-loved him.

## Other Scriptures

- **Genesis 22:1, 12** - "God tested Abraham"; the verdict is "now I know that you fear God." A test whose answer is a heart posture, not information.
- **Deuteronomy 6:4-6** - Love the LORD with all your heart; the words in your heart. The Shema puts love and law in the same sentence, then puts both in the heart. (Prior teaching: [The Shema - Deuteronomy](2025-09-14-shema-deuteronomy.md).)
- **Judges 2:22; 3:4** - God leaves nations in the land "to test Israel... whether they would obey the commandments of the LORD." Same test formula as Exodus 16 and Deuteronomy 8.
- **2 Chronicles 32:31** - God withdrew from Hezekiah "in order to test him, that He might know all that was in his heart." The withdrawal of felt presence as a test of love.
- **Job 1:9-11; 2:9-10** - Satan's thesis: love for God is really love for provision. Job's test is the beast's test in miniature.
- **Exodus 1:17** - The Hebrew midwives "feared God" over Pharaoh. The first recorded civil disobedience in Scripture is a test of whom you fear more.
- **Genesis 39:9** - Joseph: "how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?" Adultery was law before Sinai, and Joseph's refusal is framed as loyalty to God, not knowledge of a rule.
- **Esther 3:2-4** - Mordecai would not bow. A test of worship before a man's image of himself.
- **Mark 10:17-22** - The rich young ruler knew and kept the commandments from youth; "one thing you lack." He failed the provision test, exactly the buying-and-selling test from last week.
- **John 14:15, 21** - "If you love Me, keep My commandments." Jesus makes commandment-keeping the evidence of love, not the alternative to it. (Prior teaching: [Qualities of a Man of God - Guards the Commandments](2025-09-14-qualities-of-a-man-of-god-guards-the.md).)
- **John 21:15-17** - Peter's restoration exam has one question asked three times: "Do you love Me?"
- **Matthew 7:21-23** - "Lord, Lord," prophecy, and miracles, and still "I never knew you, you who practice lawlessness." Knowledge and gifting without the law kept in love.
- **James 2:19** - "Even the demons believe—and tremble!" Correct theology is not the pass mark.
- **1 Corinthians 8:1** - "Knowledge puffs up, but love edifies." The direct rebuke to a church that prides itself on knowing the symbols.
- **Romans 2:14-15** - The law written on the heart of Gentiles who never had Sinai; explains Cain, Joseph, and Abraham.
- **Psalm 26:2; Jeremiah 17:9-10** - "Try my mind and my heart"; "I, the LORD, search the heart, I test the mind." God's testing is always aimed at the heart.
- **Revelation 3:10** - "The hour of trial which shall come upon the whole world, to test those who dwell on the earth." The final test is explicitly called a test.

## Possible Closing Questions

1. **Eve could quote the command. Can you? And when you last reached for the fruit anyway, what were you loving more than God in that moment?**
2. **If God is testing "what is in your heart, whether you will keep His commandments," what has this week's test already revealed?**
3. **Daniel passed at the window because he had been at the window for years. What custom "since early days" are you building now that would hold if a decree were signed tomorrow?**
4. **Jesus told Ephesus they had tested the teachers but left their first love. If He wrote to us, which sentence would He write?**
5. **What do you know about God that has not yet become something you love about God, and what would it take to move it eighteen inches, from your head to your heart?**
