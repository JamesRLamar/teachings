# God Is Enough

> "But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts." (Romans 13:14)

I meditate on Romans 13:14 every day as a reminder to keep my heart pure. This week I realized it applies to all desire, not just lust as we usually mean it. It applies to the lust of this world.

Worldly desire takes many forms. A craving for more. A restlessness that shopping, scrolling, achieving, or being noticed never settles. There are many forms but one engine:

Doubt in God's goodness leads to discontentment.
Giving in to lust creates even more discontentment.

Several messages lately have built on the theme that "we are enough." I would add two important words: **in Him**.

When we say that we are enough, that statement is untrue if you do not add "in Him." He is the root, the source, the resilient, stable, and enduring anchor of that goodness. Without Him, we simply are not enough.

### 1. If we focus on Him, worldly desire loses its power

> "I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh." (Galatians 5:16)

We need to focus on Him. If we focus on Him and His goodness, we will not walk in worldly desire.

Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say, "Try harder not to lust, and then maybe you can walk in the Spirit." He says the opposite. Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh. Set your mind on Christ, and the earth loses its pull.

Worldly desire is not just a problem of appetite; it is a problem of attention. The heart follows the eyes, and the eyes follow whatever we keep putting in front of them.

> "You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You." (Isaiah 26:3)

A mind stayed on God is a mind that has somewhere to rest. A mind stayed on the world is a mind that is always hunting.

**How hard is it to imagine a version of yourself completely undistracted by worldly things?**

### 2. Discontentment is what grows when we stop trusting that He is enough

> "Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all that is in the world—the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life—is not of the Father but is of the world. And the world is passing away, and the lust of it; but he who does the will of God abides forever." (1 John 2:15–17)

- Doubt in God's goodness leads to discontentment. 
- Discontentment leads to lust. 
- Lust leads to even more discontentment.

John names the lust of the world in three ways: 
1. what the body desires
2. what the eyes long for
3. what the ego craves. 

> "For My people have committed two evils: They have forsaken Me, the fountain of living waters, And hewn themselves cisterns—broken cisterns that can hold no water." (Jeremiah 2:13)

God is not scolding us for being thirsty. He is grieving that we have walked away from the fountain and started digging holes in the dirt. Discontentment is not proof that God has failed you. It proves you have been drinking from a cistern that cannot hold water.

Think about a situation like this: how is this even possible?

![[thirsty-fountain.png]]


The question behind every restless pursuit is:

**Can I trust in God's goodness to be enough?**

If the answer is no, the world will always look like the solution to our heart's desire. 

As Proverbs says:

> "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life." (Proverbs 4:23)

**What does it look like to never be content? What is the fruit of that life?**

**What does it look like to be perfectly content, trusting God in all things?**

### 3. Feed the Spirit and Starve the Flesh

> "But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts." (Romans 13:14)

We can starve the lust of this world by putting on the Lord Jesus daily.

We must not forget: He is a real Person. Putting on Christ is not putting on an idea, a mood, or a costume. It is clothing ourselves in a living Spirit.

Think about what that looks like, what it feels like, to put on the Lord Jesus. Putting on perfection makes no space for lust. It makes it impossible to fulfill the lust of the flesh or the lust of the world. 

It is possible to grow closer every day—to hear Him more, know Him more, love Him more, and experience more of His goodness.

It is also possible to feed worldly desire and grow farther from Him—and more discontent. Hearing Him less. Knowing Him less. Loving Him less. Until no passion is left.

That choice is made every day when we choose first to put on the Lord Jesus, or allow ourselves to be sucked into the lust of the world.

**Describe the routine of someone totally and utterly in love with Jesus.**

**Describe the life of someone trying to ride the fence between loving Jesus and the world.**

### Closing

God is real, and He is so, so good.

I have two of the most highly credentialed and widely sought-after counselors in the world. They are counselors of counselors of counselors. Yet God is truly the wisest counselor I've ever had. I have been led by the Spirit through trauma, given wisdom impossible to know, felt His sweet presence in moments I felt lost, and sometimes just because.

I've been healed by Him, and He has healed my family. 

He's given financial provision at the perfect time too many times to remember.

When I've been lonely, He has been my friend. When I've been lost, He's been my guide. When I've been broken, He's been my healer. When I've been empty, He's been my delight.

He is good in the highest sense.

The veil is real too. Some days He feels far, but draw near anyway. Immerse yourself in His goodness if you genuinely believe He is good.

> "Nevertheless when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away... But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord." (2 Corinthians 3:16, 18)

> "Draw near to God and He will draw near to you." (James 4:8)

> "Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good; Blessed is the man who trusts in Him!" (Psalm 34:8)

> "Whom have I in heaven but You? And there is none upon earth that I desire besides You." (Psalm 73:25)

Before we ever affirm to ourselves that we are enough, we must be fully convinced that He is enough. There is no goodness apart from Him. But His goodness is enough. He is the fountain of life worth surrendering all.

Imagine someone like Jeff Bezos or Elon Musk, or pick your favorite rich person. What do you think they would pay to access a real fountain of life?

You know the fountain of Life. Now the question is, what are you giving up daily to drink from that fountain?

- Where have you been drinking from a broken cistern instead of the fountain?
- What is one way you will put on the Lord Jesus tomorrow before the world gets dressed?
- If you keep sowing what you are sowing right now, where will you be in a year—closer or colder?
