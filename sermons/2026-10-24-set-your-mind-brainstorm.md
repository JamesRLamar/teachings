# Set Your Mind - Brainstorm

## Main Idea

**Golden Apple:** Your attitude is not a mood that happens to you; it is a mind you set. The same task done with a grumble or done "from the soul" unto the Lord is decided by where you set your mind before you begin.

**One word ties all three texts together:** Paul uses the same Greek root, *phroneō* ("to set the mind, to have a mindset"), in each:

- "Let this **mind** be in you which was also in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:5), nine verses before "do all things without complaining."
- "Those who live according to the Spirit **set their minds** on the things of the Spirit... to be spiritually **minded** is life and peace" (Romans 8:5-6).
- "**Set your mind** on things above" (Colossians 3:2), the opening of the chapter that ends with "do it heartily, as to the Lord."

Spiritual mindedness is not a personality type. It is a direction you choose and keep choosing.

**Layers the message can move through:**

1. **Complaint vs. Contentment.** Grumbling is never just about the task. It reveals what we believe about God's provision.
2. **Mood vs. Mind.** Feelings report on the weather; the mind sets the temperature. We cannot always choose our feelings, but we can choose our focus.
3. **Self vs. the Lord.** Every task has an audience. When it is done "from the soul" for an Audience of One, ordinary work becomes worship.
4. **Intention vs. Rhythm.** A spiritual mind is not set once. It is set in the morning, reset at midday, and reviewed at night.

## Main Points

### 1. Grumbling is a verdict on God, not just on our circumstances

> "Do all things without complaining and disputing, that you may become blameless and harmless, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world." (Philippians 2:14-15, NKJV)

> "...for the LORD hears your complaints which you make against Him. And what are we? Your complaints are not against us but against the LORD." (Exodus 16:8, NKJV)

*Word notes:* "Complaining" (*gongysmos*) is the low, under-the-breath muttering of the crowd; it even sounds like what it means. "Disputing" (*dialogismos*) is the inner argument, the running debate in our heads about why this is unfair.

**When we grumble, we are rarely just complaining about the dishes, the boss, or the weather. We are quietly telling God He has not given us enough.**

**What do you complain about most often, and what does that complaint say about what you believe God has or has not provided?**

### 2. Attitude is set, not felt

> "For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit. For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." (Romans 8:5-6, NKJV)

**A spiritual mind is not a feeling we wait for. It is a focus we choose, and life and peace are the fruit that follows.**

**If life and peace are the fruit of a spiritual mind, what does the absence of peace in your week reveal about where your mind has been set?**

### 3. Do it from the soul, for an Audience of One

> "And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ." (Colossians 3:23-24, NKJV)

*Word notes:* "Heartily" is literally *ek psychēs*, "out of the soul." Paul wrote this to bondservants, people with the least choice over their work. If they could work "from the soul," no task is beneath worship.

**The question is not what you are doing but who you are doing it for. Changing the audience changes the attitude.**

**What task in your week do you do with the least heart, and how would it change if you pictured Jesus as the One you were doing it for?**

### 4. Set your mind daily: morning, midday, and evening

> "If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God. Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth." (Colossians 3:1-2, NKJV)

> "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly... singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord. And whatever you do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through Him." (Colossians 3:16-17, NKJV)

> "Evening and morning and at noon I will pray, and cry aloud, and He shall hear my voice." (Psalm 55:17, NKJV)

**A spiritual mind is not set once and left alone. Like a compass, it has to be checked and corrected throughout the day.**

**Practical rhythm: Set, Reset, Review.**

- **Set (morning): give God your first thought.**
  - Seek God before you check your phone (Psalm 63:1, "Early will I seek You").
  - Read one passage and carry one verse into the day (Colossians 3:16).
  - Offer the day before it starts: "Lord, everything I do today, I do for You" (Romans 12:1).
  - Name the hardest task of the day and decide ahead of time who it is for (Colossians 3:23).
- **Reset (midday): catch the grumble.**
  - When a complaint rises, take it captive (2 Corinthians 10:5). Stop and name it.
  - Turn every complaint into thanksgiving. For each grumble, name one thing to thank God for (Colossians 3:17; 1 Thessalonians 5:18).
  - Turn complaining into asking. Bring the frustration to God as a request instead of to people as a grumble (Philippians 4:6).
  - Sing or pray something short in the middle of the task (Colossians 3:16; Acts 16:25).
- **Review (evening): check the fruit.**
  - Ask, "Was today marked by life and peace?" If not, ask where your mind drifted (Romans 8:6).
  - Confess the grumbles without shame and receive mercy that is new every morning (Lamentations 3:22-23).
  - Write down one thing God did today.

**Which of these three moments, morning, midday, or evening, is the weakest in your day, and what one practice will you start this week to strengthen it?**

## Illustrations

- **Midnight in Philippi** (possible opening, maps to Points 1 and 3): The church that received "do all things without complaining" had watched Paul live it. In Acts 16, Paul and Silas were beaten, chained in stocks, and left in the inner prison of Philippi. "At midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them" (Acts 16:25). The Philippian church was born from a jail that should have been full of complaints. Paul is writing to them again from chains. He isn't asking them to do anything he hasn't done himself. *The prisoners were listening*, and the people around us are listening too.
- **Thermometer vs. Thermostat** (maps to Point 2): A thermometer only reports the temperature of the room. A thermostat sets it. A carnal mind is a thermometer; it reflects whatever mood, news, or person is in the room. A spiritual mind is a thermostat; it sets the temperature by deciding in advance where it will focus.
- **Complaining About Miracle Bread** (maps to Point 1): Israel woke up every morning to bread falling from heaven and still said, "Our whole being is dried up; there is nothing at all except this manna before our eyes!" (Numbers 11:6). Grumbling can make even a miracle feel ordinary. What "manna" in your life have you stopped seeing as a gift?
- **The Purple Bracelet Challenge** (maps to Point 4, a practical take-home): In 2006, pastor Will Bowen handed out purple bracelets and challenged people to go 21 days without complaining. Each time they complained, they switched the bracelet to the other wrist and started over. Most people needed months to make it 21 days. Consider a church-wide or family version: a rubber band, bracelet, or jar, with the twist that every switch is followed by one spoken thanksgiving.
- **Flipping the Omelet for the Love of God** (maps to Point 3): Brother Lawrence, a 17th-century monastery cook, said that he could flip an omelet in the pan for the love of God, and that his kitchen work was as much prayer as his time in chapel. The pots and pans did not change; the audience did.
- **Personal illustration slot:** A specific moment when you caught yourself grumbling about a chore, a drive, or a work task, and what changed when you reframed it as "unto the Lord." This could also be a "cracked door" type of image: the smallest complaint left unchecked lets the whole draft of a carnal mind into the room.

## Other Scriptures

- **Philippians 2:5** - "Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus"; the attitude behind "without complaining" is the mind of Christ, who humbled Himself to the cross.
- **Philippians 2:12-13** - Work out your salvation, "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do"; the right attitude is cooperation with God's work in us, not mere willpower.
- **Philippians 4:8** - "Whatever things are true... meditate on these things"; a concrete filter for what we allow to set our minds.
- **Philippians 4:11-13** - "I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content"; contentment is learned, which means it can be practiced.
- **1 Corinthians 10:10** - "Nor complain, as some of them also complained, and were destroyed"; Paul treats grumbling as a serious spiritual danger, not a harmless habit.
- **James 5:9** - "Do not grumble against one another, brethren"; complaining corrodes relationships as well as worship.
- **Isaiah 26:3** - "You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You"; the Old Testament version of Romans 8:6.
- **2 Corinthians 10:5** - Take every thought captive; the midday "reset" in practice.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:16-18** - "In everything give thanks"; thanksgiving is the direct opposite of complaining.
- **Lamentations 3:22-23** - Mercies "new every morning"; every day is a fresh opportunity to set the mind, and yesterday's failures do not have to carry over.
- **Psalm 118:24** - "This is the day the LORD has made; we will rejoice and be glad in it"; a ready-made first sentence for the morning.
- **Galatians 5:16** - "Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh"; spiritual mindedness lived out step by step.
- **Proverbs 4:23** - "Keep your heart with all diligence"; the mind requires guarding, not just good intentions.

## Possible Closing Questions

1. **If someone recorded every complaint you made this week, what would they conclude you believe about God?**
2. **Is my attitude acting more like a thermometer or a thermostat right now?**
3. **What task am I doing for myself or for people that I need to start doing for the Lord?**
4. **When was the last time I could honestly say my day was marked by life and peace, and what was my mind set on that day?**
5. **What one practice will I start tomorrow morning to set my mind before the day sets it for me?**
