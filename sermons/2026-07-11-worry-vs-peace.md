### Loosen Your Grip

> "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Philippians 4:6–7)

Worry is not a small thing. It is a strangling thing.

The Old English word for *worry*, "wyrgan," meant **to strangle**. The Latin root of "anxious, "angere," means "to choke" and "to torment." Language remembers what we forget: worry is the slow tightening of hands around a throat. We grip our lives so tightly, so afraid of losing control, that we choke the very breath meant to give life. We strangle ourselves when we try to strangle life itself, by controlling and refusing to let go and let God.

### The Broken Farmer

We've been watching Clarkson's Farm. I hilarious rollercoaster of a show about a comedian-talk-show host trying to become a farmer. We've been enjoying most of the journey until the last two episodes. Jeremy, the host, is trying to start a restaurant, a bar, a farm shop, and a butcher shop, all while harvesting his fields late into the night. Predictably, all this activity causes things to go wrong. The harvest isn't right, the restaurant is in chaos, and despite having a lot of good things going, Jeremy starts to stress and stress and stress until he winds up in a hospital just shy of a serious heart attack. It's easy to say that worry got the best of him.

In the very next episode, instead of taking stock of the good things he has and being thankful for good people and a good God who allowed him to have such plenty, Jeremy creates a nativity scene and proceeds to take every opportunity to blaspheme, degrade, and demean Christians and Christianity. It was a truly sad display of hurt, and seeing someone suffer so greatly despite having so many good things.

**Where in your life right now are you gripping so hard to stay in control that you're actually suffocating?**

### 1. Worry is the opposite of surrender

> "Martha, Martha, you are worried and troubled about many things. But one thing is needed, and Mary has chosen that good part, which will not be taken away from her." (Luke 10:41–42)

Martha wasn't sinning in some scandalous way; she was actually *serving* Jesus. But her serving had become striving, and her striving had become strangling. Jesus doesn't rebuke her work; He grieved her worry. Mary, in contrast, loosened her grip on worldly things and chose to focus on just one thing—Jesus.

**Who can you relate to more? Mary or Martha?**

### 2. Worry cannot add — it can only subtract

> "Which of you by worrying can add one cubit to his stature?... Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble." (Matthew 6:27, 34)

Jesus asks a devastatingly practical question: what has your worry ever actually accomplished? It cannot add a single hour, a single inch, a single good thing. It only borrows tomorrow's troubles to strangle today. The birds do not toil, and the lilies do not spin, yet the Father feeds and clothes them. Worry is functionally atheism, living as though there were no God in heaven who already knows what you need.

**What does it look like to let go rather than double down in trying to control things we can't control?**

**What is worry actually costing you — in sleep, in joy, in trust, in relationships?**

### 3. Peace is a choice we make with our thoughts

> "We demolish arguments and every pretension that sets itself up against the knowledge of God, and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ." (2 Corinthians 10:5)

We often treat peace as a feeling that either arrives or doesn't. But peace, as the fruit of following the Spirit, does not grow by trying harder; instead, it grows as we submit our thoughts to God. We refuse to let thoughts contrary to the truth of who God is strangle us. We take each anxious thought captive before it takes us captive.

**What are the thoughts of an anxious mind?**

**What are the thoughts of a mind at peace?**

### 4. Peace looks like sleeping through the storm

> "And a great windstorm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so that it was already filling. But He was in the stern, asleep on a pillow... Then He arose and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, 'Peace, be still!'" (Mark 4:37–39)

Thayer defines the peace of Philippians as "exemption from the rage and havoc of war." It is the peace that lets a man sleep in the stern while the storm rages, because He knows who really commands the sea. God's peace almost looks like cheating; you appear exempt from the chaos everyone else is drowning in. 

The same voice that calmed the Sea of Galilee can say to your racing heart: **Peace. Be still.**

**What common worries do you find most difficult to surrender to God? Why do you think that is?**

### Closing

Peace will never come to the soul striving to do all, be all, and control all. That soul will only suffocate. Surrender the opposite: open our hands and let God carry us, breathe life into us.

You don't have to strangle and be strangled anymore. Loosen your grip. Let go, and let Him be God.
