## Summary

Ezekiel saw a man with a writer's inkhorn go through Jerusalem marking the foreheads of those who sighed and cried over the abominations in the city. Judgment followed, and it began at the sanctuary. Revelation shows the same two-way sorting: the seal of God on the foreheads of His servants, and the mark of the beast on the hand or forehead of everyone who wants to keep buying and selling. There are only two marks, and there is no unmarked third group. The mark we bear in that day is not decided in that day. It is being written now, by whether we take God's side when it costs us reputation, money, comfort, or time. The Commandments, our courage to live those Commandments at work, our commitment to walking with God, and our committment to walk with God's people are the weekly dress rehearsal for the final test.

## Outline

1. **Opening (Ezekiel 9:1-7)** — Read the vision. Walk the room with a marker or paintbrush: "Do you sigh and cry? Are you ready for judgment?"
2. **There is no third mark (Revelation 7:2-3; 13:16-17; Deuteronomy 6:6-8)** — Both marks land on the hand and the forehead: what we do and what we think. Everyone is being marked by something.
3. **The beast's mark is a money test (Revelation 13:17; Matthew 6:24)** — Of all things, the test is buying and selling: security, comfort, provision. Do we pass that test today?
4. **The Sabbath is God's weekly rehearsal (Exodus 16:4; 31:13)** — God tested Israel with bread and rest. Guarding the Sabbath is trusting Him with provision before we are forced to.
5. **Faithful in little, faithful in much (Luke 16:10; Daniel 3:16-18)** — Courage under persecution is built in small, inconvenient stands: at work, in conversation, in the calendar.
6. **The marked are found together (Hebrews 10:24-25; Revelation 14:12)** — If the buildings closed and the internet went dark, what church would remain? Investing in God's people now is readiness.
7. **Closing (Ezekiel 9:4; 2 Corinthians 7:10)** — Return to the marker. Godly sorrow is the ink. Reflective questions and silence.

### Which Mark Do You Bear?

Revelation 14:9–11:

> "If anyone worships the beast and his image, and receives his mark on his forehead or on his hand, he himself shall also drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out full strength into the cup of His indignation. He shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels and in the presence of the Lamb. And the smoke of their torment ascends forever and ever; and they have no rest day or night, who worship the beast and his image, and whoever receives the mark of his name."

Two things are coming that the church at large is not ready for: persecution, and the judgment that follows it. Most of us assume that when the moment comes, we will rise to it. Scripture says otherwise. The mark we carry in that day is not chosen in that day. It is being written right now, in the small choices we think nobody is grading.

### The Man with the Marker and an Axe

*Read Ezekiel 9:1-7*

> "Then He called out in my hearing with a loud voice, saying, 'Let those who have charge over the city draw near, each with a deadly weapon in his hand.' And suddenly six men came from the direction of the upper gate, which faces north, each with his battle-ax in his hand. One man among them was clothed with linen and had a writer's inkhorn at his side. They went in and stood beside the bronze altar. Now the glory of the God of Israel had gone up from the cherub, where it had been, to the threshold of the temple. And He called to the man clothed with linen, who had the writer's inkhorn at his side; and the LORD said to him, 'Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and put a mark on the foreheads of the men who sigh and cry over all the abominations that are done within it.' To the others He said in my hearing, 'Go after him through the city and kill; do not let your eye spare, nor have any pity. Utterly slay old and young men, maidens and little children and women; but do not come near anyone on whom is the mark; and begin at My sanctuary.' So they began with the elders who were before the temple." (Ezekiel 9:1-7)

(Show the axe and the marker)

Notice three things about this vision. The mark was not for the religious; the slaughter began at the sanctuary, with the elders. The mark was not for the comfortable; it was for those who grieved. And the man with the inkhorn did not ask anyone what they believed. He looked for what they felt about sin.

Now hear the same scene at the end of the story:

> "Then I saw another angel ascending from the east, having the seal of the living God. And he cried with a loud voice to the four angels to whom it was granted to harm the earth and the sea, saying, 'Do not harm the earth, the sea, or the trees till we have sealed the servants of our God on their foreheads.'" (Revelation 7:2-3)

> "He causes all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, to receive a mark on their right hand or on their foreheads, and that no one may buy or sell except one who has the mark or the name of the beast, or the number of his name." (Revelation 13:16-17)

Two marks. There is no unmarked crowd in the middle.

**If the man with the marker walked through this room today, how would you be marked?**

### 1. Nobody is unmarked

> "And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up. You shall bind them as a sign on your hand, and they shall be as frontlets between your eyes." (Deuteronomy 6:6-8)

Long before the beast ever offered a mark for the hand and forehead, God had already claimed those two places. The hand is what we do. The forehead is what we think, love, and believe. Every person in this room is being marked, today, by whatever governs their hands and their minds.

That means the question is not "Will I choose a mark someday?" It is "Which mark am I already choosing?"

**We do not decide our mark on the day of judgment; we reveal the one we have been choosing all along.**

**What is currently written on your hands and your mind: God's commandments, or your own security? How would the people closest to you answer that question about you?**

### 2. The Beast's Mark is a test of provision

> "...that no one may buy or sell except one who has the mark or the name of the beast, or the number of his name." (Revelation 13:17)

> "No one can serve two masters; for either he will hate the one and love the other, or else he will be loyal to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon." (Matthew 6:24)

Look at what the final test is tied to. Not torture chambers first. Not a gun to the head first. Provison—Buying and selling. Groceries. Rent. Your job. Your ability to provide for your family. The enemy has always known that the fastest way to a man's allegiance is through his sense of security and comfort.

It is such a simple test. And it is being given to us right now, every week, in smaller doses. When keeping God's commandments costs money, which way do we lean? When honesty costs a sale, when integrity costs a promotion, when generosity costs the vacation, we are answering the question of Revelation 13 in advance.

If we will not lose a little money for God today, we will not lose our livelihood for Him then.

**The one who cannot say no to mammon now has already told the beast yes.**

**When was the last time obeying God cost you something, and you paid it gladly? If you cannot remember one, what does that tell you?**

### 3. The Sabbath is God's weekly rehearsal for that test

> "Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you. And the people shall go out and gather a certain quota every day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not.'" (Exodus 16:4)

> "Speak also to the children of Israel, saying: "Surely My Sabbaths you shall keep, for it is a sign between Me and you throughout your generations, that you may know that I am the LORD who sanctifies you."'" (Exodus 31:13)

God did not test Israel's faith with a theology exam. He tested it with bread and a day off. "Gather six days, and on the seventh, stop, and trust Me to have already provided." Some went out on the seventh day anyway, because they did not believe the sixth day's bread was enough. And God commanded them to be stoned.

God calls the Sabbath a sign between Him and His people. A sign is a mark. Every week He hands us the same test the beast will one day hand the world: Will you stop buying and selling and trust Me to provide? Guarding the Sabbath is not legalism. It is practicing for the day when trusting God with our provision will cost everything.

And it is not only the Sabbath. Do we protect our daily time with Him, or does it evaporate the moment the schedule gets tight? Daniel prayed at his window when it was illegal because he had prayed at his window every day when it was not.

**Guarding the Sabbath and our time with God is trusting Him with our provision before we are forced to.**

**Where has the Sabbath, or your daily time with God, been quietly traded for productivity, income, or convenience? What would it look like to take it back this week?**

### 4. Faithful in little, faithful in much

> "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much." (Luke 16:10)

> "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-Nego answered and said to the king, 'O Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. If that is the case, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and He will deliver us from your hand, O king. But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, nor will we worship the gold image which you have set up.'" (Daniel 3:16-18)

Those three young men did not find their courage in front of the furnace. They brought it with them. Years earlier, they had refused the king's food when refusing was merely awkward. They stood in the small, inconvenient moments, so they were able to stand in the deadly one.

This is the persecution test in its everyday form. Do we take God's side now when it costs our reputation? When it makes the conversation at work uncomfortable? When our family thinks we are strange for keeping His commandments? When it would be so much easier to stay quiet? If we shrink when the cost is embarrassment, we will not stand when the cost is prison.

**Courage under persecution is not summoned in the moment; it is built in a hundred small, inconvenient stands.**

**Name the last time you took God's side when it was inconvenient or cost you something socially. Now name the last time you stayed quiet. Which is the pattern?**

### 5. The marked assemble together

> "And let us consider one another in order to stir up love and good works, not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as is the manner of some, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching." (Hebrews 10:24-25)

> "Here is the patience of the saints; here are those who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus." (Revelation 14:12)

Notice that Hebrews ties gathering directly to the Day approaching. The closer judgment comes, the more we need each other, not less. Imagine the church building doors are shut. Imagine biblical teaching, worship music, and Christian content on the internet become illegal overnight. What would remain?

If what remains is a group of people who know each other's names, sins, and needs, who break bread in homes and pray for each other by name, then we have a church that will stand when persecution comes.

Gathering as a house church is a real buffer against institutional persecution. But it is not a shield against the daily test. A house church full of people who will not guard the Sabbath, will not speak up at work, and will not part with money for God is just a smaller room with the same problem. The marked are found together, and they are found keeping the commandments of God and holding the faith of Jesus.

**Investing our time in God's people now is how we become a church that survives when everything else is taken away.**

### Closing

The man with the inkhorn did not mark the people who attended the temple. He marked the people who sighed and cried over breaking God's Commandments. The seal of God in Revelation is on the servants of God, the ones who keep His commandments and hold the faith of Jesus. The mark of the beast is on everyone who decided their groceries mattered more than their God.

> "For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death." (2 Corinthians 7:10)

Godly sorrow is the ink. Grief over a broken relationship with God, grief over His commandments trampled, grief over the abominations we have made peace with. That grief drives us to repentance, and repentance is how the mark gets written. If nothing in us sighs and cries anymore, we should be alarmed.

You are already choosing your mark. Every inconvenient obedience is the pen. Every Sabbath guarded, every dollar surrendered, every uncomfortable stand, every evening given to God's people instead of a screen, is a stroke of the seal of the living God on your forehead. Every quiet compromise is a stroke of the other one.

- Do you sigh and cry over sin to the ppoint that you actually walk away from it rather than back to it?
- Do you take God's side now, in the small things?
- Is the Sabbath, and your daily time with God, protected, or is it the first thing traded when inconvenient?
- How well have you invested yourself in this community? How well do you know and are known by others?
- If God walked through this room today, which mark would he find you already wearing?
