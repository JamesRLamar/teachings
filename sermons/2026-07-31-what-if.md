### What If?

![[What If.png]]

Two words. 

"What if...?"

That's a powerful question, because it can warp the very fabric of your reality. While Thanos needed six Infinity Stones to unmake half of the universe, you can undo your whole world with just those two words—and never even speak out loud.

Today we're going to examine both edges of that question: the edge that destroys you and the edge that sets you free.

### 1. The dread-filled "What if...?" is a dark prophecy over yourself

> "But the men who had gone up with him said, 'We are not able to go up against the people, for they are stronger than we.' And they gave the children of Israel a bad report of the land which they had spied out... and we were like grasshoppers in our own sight, and so we were in their sight." (Numbers 13:31–33)

> "Why has the LORD brought us to this land to fall by the sword, that our wives and children should become victims?" (Numbers 14:3)

The ten spies didn't lie about the giants. The giants were real. The mistake they made was assuming the worst. They took a true fact and finished the story without God in it: **what if** we all die, **what if** our wives are taken, **what if** our children become plunder. All of Israel was swept up in the dread-filled "What if...?".

Notice the fatal line: "we were like grasshoppers in our own sight." They were completely focused on themselves, and their self-image became incredibly small. From this story we learn that a dread-filled "What if...?" will break us before we've even seen real battle.

What are some of the worst-case scenarios you've imagined that caused you great pain or worry, only never to have come true?

### 2. Underneath every dread-filled "What if...?" is the lie that you are alone

Elijah was a prophet of God who stood against the evil King Ahab and almost 1,000 of his bloodthirsty false prophets. Just after calling down fire from heaven and slaying the prophets of Baal, Elijah collapsed under a broom tree and complained, "I alone am left; and they seek to take my life." (1 Kings 19:10).

God replied: "Yet I have reserved seven thousand in Israel, all whose knees have not bowed to Baal." (1 Kings 19:18) 

Elijah was off by seven thousand. 

He wasn't wrong about the danger; he was just wrong about being alone.

Consider this picture of someone who is feeling crushed over some loss or heartache. It's desperate, broken, and lonely.

![[before 1.png]]

But consider what happens when we reframe our perspective and include God in the picture. 

![[after.png]]

God is so much more than a cheesy Jesus hand on our shoulder. The Scriptures claim, “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.” (Psalm 46:1)

Think about your regular worries and fears. What could change today if you genuinely believed you were not alone in your struggles?

### 3. Your mind will camp wherever you aim it

> "You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You." (Isaiah 26:3)

> "For he is never shaken; the righteous is remembered forever. He is not afraid of an evil report. His heart is steadfast, trusting in Yahweh. His heart is upheld, he is not afraid, while he looks on his adversaries." (Psalm 112:6–8)

The Hebrew behind the word "stayed" in Isaiah 26:3 carries the sense of leaning, propped, supported: a mind resting its full weight on God like a man leaning on a staff. 

And notice Psalm 112 does not say the righteous man never hears an evil report. He hears it. He simply isn't afraid of it. He's also not delusional. He acknowledges reality, but he's fair to invite an Almighty God into that reality as well. He doesn't just look at his adversaries; he looks straight through them.

There is real consequence behind imagined stress. Researchers at Penn State established that the brain does not distinguish well between a rehearsed catastrophe and a real one; the same stress response fires either way, which is why worry costs you sleep, health, and years of life over events that never arrive. Yet the very same imaginative machinery, framed with God in the picture, gives us courage, endurance, and vision.

It's the "What if...?" question, and the crux is our aim.

Where do you tend to aim your "What if...?" questions? At Jesus or your perceived problems?

### 4. The faith-filled "What if...?" asks God-sized questions

> "If God is for us, who can be against us? (Romans 8:31)

> "Now to Him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that works in us..." (Ephesians 3:20)

> "We demolish arguments and every pretension that sets itself up against the knowledge of God, and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ." (2 Corinthians 10:5)

> "Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth." (Colossians 3:2)

Returning to the opening story of the 12 spies. 10 focused on a bad report and suffered in a wilderness of their own making for 40 years! 

The two spies who came back with a different report, Joshua and Caleb, saw the same giants, but they reversed the "What if...?" question. They asked: what if the same God who brought us out of Egypt is with us to bring us into the Promised Land?

Peter did this on the Sea of Galilee. He asked a faith-filled "What if...?": "Lord, if it is You, command me to come to You on the water"—and then he walked on water!

But in the same story it says, "when he saw that the wind was boisterous, he was afraid," and he sank (Matthew 14:28–31). Same sea. Same storm. Same moment. The only thing that changed was his perspective. When he studied the wind and waves, he forgot who was with him; he framed God out of the picture.

What is one dread-filled "What if...?" you've been rehearsing this week, and what does it look like to reframe your situation to include God?

### Closing

When I was 13, I was lost and depressed in a way I didn't have words for. And I was not thinking about taking my life; I was in the act of committing suicide when the question seized me: What if...?

What if God is real? What if heaven and hell are real? What if I die and spend forever in hell? That one question stopped me from ending my life. That one question set me on a trajectory to knowing God and actually finding life.

That might seem like a dread-filled "What if...?", and it was, but it was a holy dread. Sometimes we need to be willing to face that kind of question. For there is no point in pretending that if God is not real, what point is there in reframing anything? If God's not real, then we are alone; and that is the truth of it.

Blaise Pascal, mathematician and physicist, known for the Pascal unit of pressure among other things, framed the idea of believing in God as plain arithmetic: "If you gain, you gain all; if you lose, you lose nothing. Wager, then, without hesitation that He is." 

He's saying that if you bet your life on God and you're wrong, you have lost nothing. But if you bet your life against Him and you're wrong, you have lost everything. 

Jesus didn't leave us in the realm of odds and hedging bets. He didn't leave room for Allah, or Buddha, or you, or me to be God. He said it plainly:

> "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me." (John 14:6)

So, what if?

What if Jesus is exactly who He says He is?

What if living for our own selfish desires carries the severest consequence there is?

What if a living God, infinitely good, is standing just on the other side of our repentance, waiting to hand us eternal life?

What if life is not as long as we're hoping? What if that reckoning is closer than we think?

What if...?
