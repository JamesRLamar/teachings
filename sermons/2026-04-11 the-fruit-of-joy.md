### The Fruit of Joy

> "My brethren, count it all **joy** when you fall into various trials, knowing that the testing of your faith produces patience. **But let patience have its perfect work**, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking nothing." (James 1:2-4)

Joy may be the most misunderstood fruit of the Spirit. We often assume it means the absence of things that make us feel bad, but Scripture never promises that. Instead, it offers something surprising: a gladness that grows *through* the trial rather than apart from it. 
 
### My Terrible Boss

This week, my boss was amazingly difficult—again. It wasn’t just a rough day; it was the kind of tension that wrecks your sleep and takes days to recover from. My mind spiraled into worst-case scenarios: *What if this never gets better? What if this anxiety gives me another ulcer? What if this costs me my job?*

Then, right in the middle of that noise, a still, small thought broke through: *What if this struggle is exactly where I need to be?*

Not because the struggle is good in itself, but because God is in it with me. I thought differently: *What if He’s using this to shape me? Maybe even bless me?* 

When I finally surrendered to that and let go of my need to control—something shifted. I found the courage to go to my boss and address the issue, not with resentment, but with grace. And you know what? He received it well.

Yet the most important part wasn’t his response. I was genuinely joyful before I knew how it would turn out. I had already settled it in my mind: if things got worse, God would simply have to intervene all the more. I would grow all the more. I would flourish all the more. The outcome couldn’t touch my joy because my joy wasn’t built on it.

### 1. Joy Is a Perspective, Not a Feeling

> "My brethren, count it all **joy** when you fall into various trials..." (James 1:2-4)

**Joy is a way of seeing—a perspective that can look at the dirt and still see the harvest.**

The word “count” here translates the Greek word hēgeomai, which means to consider, to think, to lead with your mind. James is not commanding us to feel happy; he is calling us to think about our situation differently. Joy is an action of intention. We look at the trial and intentionally declare: this has value, because God is at work in it.

That is why joy cannot be circumstantial. Happiness is tied to what happens; joy is rooted in what you know. And what do you know? That God “works all things together for good for those who love Him and are called according to His purpose.” (Romans 8:28)

### 2. Joy Is Rooted in the Presence of God

> "You will show me the path of life; in Your presence is fullness of **joy**; at Your right hand are pleasures forevermore." (Psalm 16:11)

> "Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines; though the labor of the olive may fail, and the fields yield no food; though the flock may be cut off from the fold, and there be no herd in the stalls — yet I will **rejoice** in the Lord, I will **joy** in the God of my salvation." (Habakkuk 3:17-18)

Habakkuk’s prayer is staggering. Everything has failed: the crops, the livestock, the economy, the future. Everything is barren. Yet his response is, "I will rejoice." This is not denial. This is a man who has located his joy somewhere that circumstances cannot find.

Notice what he does not say: "I will enjoy failure." Instead, he uses *joy* like a verb—an action to take, not an outcome to wait for. He delights in the true treasure, which the losses of this world cannot touch. 

If we believe our treasure is on earth, we will collapse under the pressure of trials. Christ is the treasure, and the fullest experience of that treasure is still ahead. We truly have much to anticipate.

Psalm 16:11 shows us where this joy is found: in His presence. Joy does not come from effort or positive thinking; it is found in the atmosphere of being near God. When we are rooted in His presence—as we talked about last week—joy will come. And the opposite is just as true: if we are not in His presence, we will not have joy. It cannot be manufactured apart from Him. Plain and simple.

### 3. Joy Produces Courage and Strength

> "And not only that, but we also **glory** in tribulations, knowing that tribulation produces perseverance; and perseverance, character; and character, hope. Now hope does not disappoint..." (Romans 5:3-5)

**Why doesn't hope disappoint?** (We'll come back to this.)

Notice that Paul uses the word *glory* as a verb here, much like Habakkuk uses the word *joy*. In this sense, they’re pointing to the same kind of posture.

So if joy is a perspective, then what is the fruit of joy? What does joy actually produce in a person’s life?

Joy produces strength. Not the grin-and-bear-it, fake-it-till-you-make-it kind, but the kind that makes you walk into a hard conversation with grace instead of fear; that helps you smile at those who gossip about you; that keeps you steady when the people around you are losing their minds.

This became real for me this week. My anxiety only broke when joy entered, and joy only entered when I stopped fighting the trial and started trusting our Father’s counsel. I began by recognizing how full I was of my own thoughts and how absent His thoughts were. Then I started to worship. I felt His presence slowly fill my heart. I sat in silence for a moment and listened—and wouldn’t you know, I heard Him speak.

He began reshaping my perspective, just as we’ve been talking about. As I meditated, my soul shifted. Anxiety became quieter, then eventually, joy. I went from being trapped and terrified to actually feeling excited to confront my boss.

**Why doesn’t hope disappoint?**

Because hope is the expectation of good. Hope is knowing God is always good.

### Closing

Joy is the settled decision to see God's hand in every circumstance and to trust that His purposes are good, even when the evidence seems absent. Joy is rooted in His presence, refined through trials, and revealed in the courage and strength it produces.

If your joy has felt thin lately, the way forward is not to try harder to be happy, but to draw closer to the source of joy Himself. Abide in Him. Surrender. Sink your roots deep into His love. Then allow the trial to do its work. Wait and see how God shows up. First in you, and maybe even in your situation.

- What is currently killing your joy? Name it honestly before God.
- Are you being controlled by fear?
- In the most stressful area of your life right now, what do you expect to see next week—evil or good? And does it ultimately matter what “happens” if you choose to trust Him and refuse to yield to fear?
