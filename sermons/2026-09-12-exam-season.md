## Test of Trumpets

What are we looking for in the sky?

What is the symbol of Christ's return?

Yesterday, I sent you a short reminder about the Feast of Trumpets, and I didn't have to explain any of those answers because I knew you knew. It's great that you know. I went to post the same exact thing on FB, and Aimee reminded me that most people wouldn't have a clue what I'm talking about. Then John Bevere, a teacher I respect a lot, put up a post teaching everything you guys just said. You could have taught John's lesson!

Yet the test of Trumpets, the real day it foreshadows, is not a test of the mind, but a test of the heart. Keeping God's commandments is an outward expression of an inward condition. And more than anything, I want the Church to be prepared for that exam season. And I want us to be prepared.

> "And you shall remember that the LORD your God led you all the way these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you and **test** you, to know what was in your **heart**, whether you would keep His **commandments** or not." (Deuteronomy 8:2)

### 1. The True Test of the Church

> "I know your works, your labor, your patience, and that you cannot bear those who are evil. And you have tested those who say they are apostles and are not, and have found them liars... Nevertheless I have this against you, that you have left your first love." (Revelation 2:2-4)

> "And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge... but have not love, I am nothing." (1 Corinthians 13:2)

Ephesus was the doctrinally sharpest church in Revelation. They tested teachers. They knew their symbols. They could have taught the trumpets. And Jesus says, "I have this against you." The final test in Revelation is worship (13:15), provision (13:17), and keeping the commandments of God (14:12). Not one of those is a knowledge test.

They got a perfect score in prophecy but failed the only test that really mattered: love.

**What would Jesus write to the Church in America?**

**What would Jesus write to you?**

### 2. The Test of the Commandments Before Sinai

> "So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate." (Genesis 3:6)

The first test in history was a test of the First and Greatest Commandment, to love God with all our heart.

> "So the LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry? And why has your countenance fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin lies at the door...'" (Genesis 4:6-7)

> "...not as Cain who was of the wicked one and murdered his brother. And why did he murder him? Because his works were evil and his brother's righteous." (1 John 3:12)

> "...because Abraham obeyed My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes, and My laws." (Genesis 26:5)

> "Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you... that I may test them, whether they will walk in My law or not.'" (Exodus 16:4)

**If we can quote the command, but still reach for the fruit, what does that reveal about what our true love?**

**What does this test look like today?**

### 3. The Mark of the Beast Foreshadowed

> "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abed-Nego answered and said to the king, 'O Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. If that is the case, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace... But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, nor will we worship the gold image which you have set up.'" (Daniel 3:16-18)

> "Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went home. And in his upper room, with his windows open toward Jerusalem, he knelt down on his knees three times that day, and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as was his custom since early days." (Daniel 6:10)

These were tests of the first and second commandments, putting God first and only and refusing to worship idols.

And notice it says of Daniel "as was his custom since early days." Loving God in secret is how you pass the exam in public.

- In AD 112, the governor Pliny wrote to Emperor Trajan explaining his method for identifying Christians: he had them invoke the gods by offering wine and incense before the emperor's image
- In 151 AD, the bishop of Rome, Victor, challenged that any church that kept the biblical Passover was to be excommunicated, anathema. 
- Polycarp traveled to meet Victor to confront him, to no avail. A few years later, in 155 AD, he was taken prisoner and forced to declare "Caesar is Lord" or be burned alive. He would not and was killed on the biblical day of Passover.
- The Council of Laodicea (AD 364) declared anathema on Christians who "rest on the Sabbath."
- In AD 787, the Second Council of Nicaea, under the authority of the empire, mandated that Christians worship images of saints and declared those who did not to be anathema and excommunicated.

**If a law were signed tomorrow that said, by penalty of death, we must pray to the president, or that we must kiss the feet of some statue, or that we must work on the seventh day, or that you must support the murder of an innocent human being, how many Christians would bow?**

>For whoever shall keep the whole law, and yet stumble in one point, he is guilty of all. For He who said, “Do not commit adultery,” also said, “Do not murder.” Now if you do not commit adultery, but you do murder, you have become a transgressor of the law. So speak and so do as those who will be judged by the law of liberty. (James 2:10-12)

**What good is it if you can quote the Commandments but don't keep them?**

**What good is it if you rest on the Sabbath but give in to lust?**

**What meaning is there if you attend Church every week but don't meet with Jesus every day?**
