# The Good Soldier

> “You therefore must endure hardship as a good soldier of Jesus Christ. No one engaged in warfare entangles himself with the affairs of this life, that he may please him who enlisted him as a soldier.” (2 Timothy 2:3-4)

Every soldier who has held the line in a hard place did so for one reason: belief in what they were fighting for. Give someone something worth dying for, and they can endure anything. Take that reason away, and **surrender comes before the battle even begins.**

### Two Soldiers, One Trench

Picture two soldiers in the same cold trench, enduring the same rain, hunger, and enemy. One is defeated before the battle begins; the other is ready for war. The difference is *vision*. The defeated man sees only his suffering and the pain of death. The ready man sees beyond the ridge: home, loved ones, and victory. Same trench, same suffering—two entirely different perspectives.

This is the essence of the Christian life. We are all in the trench. Trials come to each of us, sometimes slow and grinding, sometimes sharp and costly. The question is not whether we will suffer, but whether we can still see Jesus when we do.

He, too, fixed His eyes on a prize: us—broken, afraid, and lost. Our redemption was His joy set before Him. He endured because He believed in what He was fighting for. So must we.

**When suffering comes, what will you focus on: the cold, wet mud at your feet, the enemy’s hateful gaze, or the prize waiting on the other side of this war?**

### 1. The Soldier Knows Where to Set Their Focus

> “…Let us run with endurance the race that is set before us, looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith, who for the joy that was set before Him endured the cross, despising the shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.” (Hebrews 12:1-2)

**Endurance comes not from strength or grit, but from focus.**

What was the prize Jesus saw? His joy was not escape from pain, but *us*. He looked beyond the cross and saw the broken made whole, captives set free, the lost brought home. He saw you, no longer a slave to shame, but redeemed. That vision made the cross endurable. Paul calls our affliction “light” and “momentary” because it’s weighed against an “eternal weight of glory” we see only when we lift our eyes from the present.

> “For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory, while we do not look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen. For the things which are seen are temporary, but the things which are not seen are eternal.” (2 Corinthians 4:17-18)

> “If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God. Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth.” (Colossians 3:1-2)

**What is the joy/prize set before us? Can you name it, see it, or have your eyes drifted down to the mud at your feet?**

### 2. The Soldier Endures Because He Believes in the Cause

> “You therefore must endure hardship as a good soldier of Jesus Christ… that he may please him who enlisted him as a soldier.” (2 Timothy 2:3-4)

**A good soldier endures because he believes in the cause. Conviction transforms suffering—it forges us instead of breaking us.**

**But not all endurance is holy. Sometimes, we fear not suffering, but disappointment:** letting others down, falling short, failing to live up to an image. We grind and suffer, but for the wrong reasons.

The soldier is called to endure “that he may please *him who enlisted him*”.

How do we know if our cause is worthy?

### 3. The Soldier Knows What to Cling to

> “Cling to discipline. Do not relax your grip on it. Keep it because it is your life.” (Proverbs 4:13, GW)

What disciplines help us keep our eyes on the prize?

> “No one engaged in warfare entangles himself with the affairs of this life…” (2 Timothy 2:4)

> “For whoever desires to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake will find it.” (Matthew 16:24-25)

And here is the danger for us: the things that entangle us are usually not sins. They are good things, ordinary things that have quietly grown heavy enough to pull our eyes off God.

**What are the “affairs of this life” that might distract us from the prize?**

**What does it say about our gaze, what love and value, if we are distracted by earthly things?**

### Closing

Two soldiers, one trench. Vision or lack of it. That is the whole difference in the Christian life.

Jesus could see us through the cross. The question is whether we can still see Him through our suffering.

- When you endure, who are you really enduring for—the One who enlisted you, or the image of yourself you are afraid to lose?
- What hardship in your life this week could you endure well if you could clearly see the prize set before you?
- What is one “affair of this life,” even a good one, that you sense God asking you to loosen your grip on?
- What are you trying to save that Jesus is asking you to let die?
