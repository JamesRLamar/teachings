> Then there appeared to them divided tongues, as of fire, and one sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance. (Acts 2:1–4)

### Hoist The Sail

What good is a sailboat if there is no wind to power it across the sea?

Sailboats have rudders that are designed to provide direction. But a sailboat was not built to move by rudder alone. It was built to catch the wind.

This is a picture of the Christian life. We need direction of the Spirit, the ability to hear His voice. We need the fruit of the Spirit. But we are not meant to steer ourselves across the sea of life by our own strength. We were made to be filled with the Spirit and empowered for the work of God.

**Have you been trying to follow Jesus with a rudder but no wind?**

### 1. Be Filled With The Spirit

> "And do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation; but be filled with the Spirit." (Ephesians 5:18)

**To be filled with the Spirit is to come under His holy influence until our lives begin to carry His character and power.**

We have just talked about the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. The fruit of the Spirit shows us the character of a Spirit-filled life.

The question is not only, "Do I have the Spirit?" but "Does the Spirit have me?"

A sailboat can have a sail and still not catch the wind. A believer can belong to the Spirit and still resist the fullness of the Spirit. To be filled is to hoist the sail. It is to yield our lives, our words, our minds, our hands, and our availability to the Spirit of God.

### 2. Eagerly Desire The Gifts Of The Spirit

> "Pursue love, and **desire** spiritual gifts, but especially that you may prophesy." (1 Corinthians 14:1)

**The Bible does not tell us to tolerate spiritual gifts, but to desire them in love.**

For many of us, this may feel unfamiliar. Some did not grow up around the spiritual gifts. Some have seen gifts misused, exaggerated, or performed in ways that felt strange or unwise. The Bible never asks us to embrace confusion, pride, manipulation, or disorder, but we must be careful not to reject the gift because someone mishandled it.

> "Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies. Test all things; hold fast what is good." (1 Thessalonians 5:19–21)

To reject the gifts of the Spirit is not merely to reject a charismatic style. It is to refuse something God commands us to desire. It is to tell the body of Christ, "We do not need that grace." It is to leave the sail lowered while asking why the boat feels powerless.

**Why are we afraid of seeking the gifts of the Spirit?**

**What does it look like to hoist your sail?**

### 3. We NEED the Spirit

> "Then Moses said to him, 'Are you zealous for my sake? Oh, that all the LORD's people were prophets and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!'" (Numbers 11:29)

**Moses saw a day when God's presence would not rest on one leader only, but would overflow onto all God's people.**

These men were the seventy elders of Israel who led over a million people, and yet God knew they needed more.

> "And it shall come to pass in the last days, says God, That I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh; Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, Your young men shall see visions, Your old men shall dream dreams. And on My menservants and on My maidservants I will pour out My Spirit in those days; And they shall prophesy." (Acts 2:17-18)

On Pentecost, the Spirit was poured out on the 120 disciples who had cast out demons, healed the sick, and spent 3.5 years walking and talking with Jesus, yet God knew they needed more.

> When the Day of Pentecost had fully come, they were all with one accord in one place...And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit... (Acts 2:1–4)

The gifts of the Spirit include:

- wisdom
- knowledge
- faith
- healing
- miracles
- prophecy
- discernment
- heavenly language and divine interpretation
- serving
- teaching
- generosity
- leadership
- mercy
- and more

What would it look like if those gifts were being used every week?

What if our problem was that so many people had dreams, visions, prophetic words, testimonies of healing, demonic deliverance, etc., that we had to order things just so we had time for teaching, prayer, and worship?

### 4. How to Receive the Spirit

We receive the fullness of the Spirit by coming to the Father through faith in Jesus, repenting of sin, surrendering ourselves fully, asking boldly, and welcoming the Spirit’s work with obedient faith.

1. They believe the gospel and receive Christ.  In Acts 10, Cornelius and his household receive the Spirit while Peter is preaching Christ.
  > “While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell upon all those who heard the word.”  Acts 10:44
2. They repent and are baptized. Peter connects repentance, baptism, forgiveness, and the reception of the Spirit.
  > “Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.”  Acts 2:38
3. They pray and wait on God. The disciples waited in obedience until the Spirit came at Pentecost.
  > “These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication…”  Acts 1:14
4. They ask the Father. Jesus directly encourages His people to ask for the Holy Spirit.
  > “How much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”  Luke 11:13
5. They receive through the laying on of hands. In Acts 8 and Acts 19, believers receive the Spirit when apostles lay hands on them.
  > “Then they laid hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit.”  Acts 8:17
6. They continue being filled. Ephesians 5:18 is present/ongoing: “be being filled.” This is not just a one-time moment but a continual life of yielding to the Spirit.

There isn't one mechanism to being filled with the Spirit. It is simple, but I understand that for many, it may not be easy.

**Unfortunately, for many, God is more willing to fill us than we are willing to be filled.**

### Closing Thoughts

The Christian life is not a rowboat powered by human hands. He has given us a sail, and He has commanded us to be filled with the Spirit.

- Is your sail still down?
- Have I confused caution with obedience?
- What does it say about our trust in God if we are more afraid of His gifts than hungry for His help?
- Have I rejected gifts God commands me to desire?
- Do I need to ask the Holy Spirit to fill me? Maybe again?
- What gift might God want to give through me?
- If you are afraid God won't answer your prayer, stop asking, "What if He doesn't give me His Spirit?" Start asking: What if He does?
